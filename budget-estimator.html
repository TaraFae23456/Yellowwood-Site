Why these numbers

Calibrated, not guessed.

Most short-term rental design firms either hide their pricing entirely or publish a single vague number (a "$3,500 per space" rule of thumb that breaks down the moment your property has more than three spaces). Yellowwood Atelier publishes the math.

The Editorial-tier benchmarks driving this estimator

  • Per bedroom: $2,700–$6,800 in supplier cost — covering bed frame and mattress, soft goods (linens, duvet, throw pillows, throws), bedroom décor (lamps, art, rug, accents), and curtains
  • Per common-area square foot: $13.50–$37 — covering sofas, dining table and chairs, coffee and side tables, lighting, art, rugs, accents, and curtains
  • Per bathroom: $700–$2,600 — covering bath linens, décor, accent storage, and any required mirrors or lighting upgrades
  • Kitchen tools and tabletop: $2,300–$5,500 per property — typically the most underbudgeted category (cookware, knives, espresso, blender, stand mixer, dishes, glassware, flatware, serveware)
  • Wallpaper and wall treatments: $0–$3,200 per property — scales with property size; the highest-leverage editorial move per dollar
  • Per outdoor zone: $3,800–$14,400 — outdoor furniture, lighting, rug, hot tub or fire-pit accessories
  • Hospitality and amenities: $1,550–$4,300 per property — welcome basket, scent profile, books, in-property guidebook, signage

What's not captured in the estimate

The estimator returns furnishing supplier cost. It doesn't include design fees (Yellowwood Atelier or any designer), structural work, custom millwork, architectural collaboration, photography production beyond standard install, or property management fees. For a complete project budget, plan to layer those on top.

Common questions

What owners ask about STR furnishing costs.

How much does it cost to furnish a short-term rental?
An Editorial-tier furnishing budget for a 3-bedroom short-term rental typically runs $55,000–$95,000 in supplier cost, plus 22% for install, freight, and photography (~$12,000–$20,000), plus state sales tax (5–10%). All-in furnishing project total: roughly $75,000–$130,000 for a 3BR property. Studio Stays (1–2BR) run $40,000–$70,000 all-in. Estates (5BR+) run $150,000–$280,000 all-in. Use the calculator above for a property-specific estimate.
How much should I budget per bedroom for an Airbnb?
At Editorial tier, plan for $2,700–$6,800 in supplier cost per bedroom — covering bed frame and mattress ($1,500–$3,500), soft goods and linens ($400–$800), bedroom décor like lamps and art ($600–$1,200), and curtains ($200–$500). The primary suite typically runs higher than secondary bedrooms because of additional seating and accents.
What's the kitchen budget for a vacation rental?
Kitchen tools and tabletop are commonly underbudgeted by 30–50%. At Editorial tier, plan for $2,300–$5,500 across cookware, knives, small appliances (espresso, blender, stand mixer), dishes, glassware, flatware, and serveware. Editorial-tier short-term rentals carry two-times guest-count minimums for tabletop — guests photograph and review the kitchen as much as the bedroom.
Do I need wallpaper for my Airbnb?
Wallpaper is one of the highest-leverage editorial moves for short-term rentals. A floor-to-ceiling powder-room wallpaper, a single accent bedroom wall, or a dining-ceiling treatment generates outsized social-media and listing-photo impact at modest cost ($0–$3,200 per property at Editorial tier). It's the line item that most often distinguishes a designed STR from an undesigned one in photographs.
What additional costs sit on top of FF&E?
Plan for an additional 22% on top of supplier cost for install, freight, and photography production. State sales tax adds 5–10% depending on jurisdiction. So for a $70,000 supplier-cost project, the all-in furnishing total is typically $90,000–$95,000 once install, freight, photo, and tax are added.

What's the difference between Standard, Editorial, and Luxury tier furnishing?
Standard tier (~65% of Editorial pricing) sources from Wayfair, Target, IKEA, and budget brands — appropriate for entry-level STRs. Editorial tier is the Yellowwood Atelier baseline — Castlery, Albany Park, Sundays, Schoolhouse, P.F. Candle Co., Brooklinen — properties that photograph well and command premium ADR. Luxury tier (~165% of Editorial) sources from RH, 1stDibs, custom commissions, and designer-trade vendors — reserved for properties commanding $1,000+/night and white-glove hospitality.

How do I estimate common-area square footage if I don't have a floor plan?
Common areas typically equal 30–40% of a property's total interior square footage. A 1,800 sqft house generally has 540–720 sqft of common space (living, kitchen, dining, foyer combined). The estimator includes a "Estimate from total sqft" mode that does this conversion for you. If the listing publishes total square footage but not room-by-room dimensions, that's the fastest path to a working estimate.
What's the install and freight cost on top of furnishing?
Plan for 18–25% on top of supplier cost. This breaks down as: receiving and quality-control labor ($500–$2,000), consolidated freight (8–15% of supplier cost), local install crew over 2–3 days ($1,500–$4,500), and photography production for the launch shoot ($1,500–$4,000). State sales tax adds 5–10% on top of furnishing supplier cost depending on jurisdiction.
