The owners came to Yellowwood Atelier with a 3-bedroom cabin that was already built, already on Airbnb, and already underperforming. $175 ADR. 36% occupancy. Below market median in both directions, on a property that should have cleared $300 a night without much effort.

The problem wasn't the cabin. The cabin was fine. The problem was that nothing about the listing photographed differently from the four hundred other 3-bedroom cabins in Hochatown.

When a market is this saturated, the only listing that wins is the one that looks like a different category of property.

That's the engagement Yellowwood Atelier was hired to deliver. Full interior redesign plus a brand the property never had: name, identity, in-property applications, direct-booking site visual style. A complete repositioning of an existing asset.

The design story

An equestrian retreat,
in a cabin in Oklahoma.

"Rustic. Horses. Refined. Private. With a bit of unexpected funk." That was the brief, written on the first page of the Canva concept board.

Most Broken Bow cabins commit to "cabin" and stop there. Wood walls, leather, antlers. The math says it should work. The math is also why every cabin in the market is interchangeable.

Gilded Spur was designed against that. The reference set: heritage equestrian, refined western fine art, layered leather, and a Sherwin-Williams palette anchored by Antiquarian Brown with Dard Hunter Green as the deep accent. Painted blue cabinetry against pine. A custom horse mural in the great room treated like a gallery canvas, not a commercial print. Vintage duck decoys on the chimney column. Cowhide rugs, leather sectional, hand-painted horse triptych in the primary bath.

Outside, the brief got bigger. The wellness circuit (hot tub, sauna, cold plunge) and the covered pool bar with green-and-white striped awning push the property into a different ICP entirely. Couples on weekend retreats. Wellness-focused families. Double-date trips. Adults who want a hideaway, not a noisy lake cabin.

The Equestrian-Luxury concept made it possible to ask $437 a night because the property no longer competes with $239 cabins. It competes with boutique hotels.

Same cabin.
Different property.

The "before" photos came from the prior Airbnb listing. The "after" photos were shot by GracieLuxe Co after the Yellowwood Atelier rebuild. Same address. Same square footage. The backyard is where the engagement made the most aggressive moves. Inside, every room was rebuilt as a moment.

The backyard. The original listing called the yard a feature. The Atelier engagement built the backyard into the property: in-ground pool, covered pool bar, full wellness circuit, hammocks, fire pit, cornhole. This is the photograph guests post first.

From the other side. Cedar-barrel sauna, cold plunge, pool, pool bar, hot tub, and a fenced perimeter that lets guests use every zone at once.

The great room, wide. The cabinetry stayed. The chandelier, brass, leather, marble, and the painted-mural wall changed the room's whole category.

The mural wall. Same blank shiplap, transformed by Magic Hat Murals (Jes Weiner) into the property's defining art moment. The wall guests stand in front of first.

The primary bedroom. Same room, same barn door. The four-poster steel bed, oxblood velvet, equestrian-print drapes, and the "under the Oklahoma sky with you" neon turned a generic primary into the listing's most-photographed interior moment.

The second bedroom. Same room, same wood walls. New bed, new lighting, new layering — every secondary bedroom in the property gets the same level of attention as the primary.

The loft, in use. Shuffleboard, neon, sleeper sofa, slant-wall mural. The blank upstairs room reimagined as the adult game lounge.

The loft, second angle. The hand-painted slant-wall mountain mural anchors the room. Arcade, custom bunks, neon, cowhide rug.

The kids' room. Reframed from a plain bunk room into a small cabin-within-a-cabin, layered with the same equestrian language as the rest of the property.
